# Copy method

Every word on the page is for a customer deciding whether to trust this business. Write
like the owner would if they were good at it: plain, specific, confident, local.

## Principles
- Clear over clever. If a line needs a second read, rewrite it.
- Benefits over features. "Delivered before 3pm" beats "Fast delivery service".
- Specific over vague. Numbers, names, places, times. "Baked at 6am and 4pm", not
  "fresh daily". Never invent statistics, awards or testimonials; when the spec gives
  none, leave them out.
- Customer language over company language. Say what they would say to a friend.
- One idea per section, one sentence per idea where possible.

## The hero
- Headline: the outcome for the customer, 3 to 8 words, no brand name (the wordmark
  already says it). Formulas: "{Outcome} without {pain}", "{Product} for {audience}",
  "{Question about their pain}", "{Concrete promise}, {where or when}".
- Subheadline: one or two sentences that add the specifics the headline left out: what,
  for whom, how, how much, from where.
- Primary button: verb + what they get. "Book a slot", "Order a box", "See all pairs".
  Never "Submit", "Sign up", "Learn more", "Get started", "Click here".
- A proof line under the buttons when there is a true fact: "12 pairs in stock, from
  ₦138,000", "Open today until 7pm".

## Sections
- Section titles are statements or questions, not labels. "Bags in the workshop now",
  not "Products". "Three messages, one warm bag", not "How to order".
- A one-line lead under each title, then the content. No intro paragraph that repeats
  the title.
- Product and service names read like a real catalogue: specific model or variant, a
  short line on what makes this one different, the price formatted (₦12,000).
- Trust items are facts with a number or a condition: "Free repairs in year one", not
  "Quality guaranteed".
- About: how it started, in two or three sentences with a place and a year; what the
  owner refuses to compromise on. No mission statements.
- Empty states and errors: say what happened and what to do next, in one line.
- Footer: name, one line about the business, address, hours, phone, email. Real details
  from the spec; if the spec gives none, use a plausible Lagos address and a 080 number
  and say nothing false about awards or years.

## Sounding human, not generated
Cut all of these:
- Throat-clearing: "Welcome to", "Discover", "Introducing", "Here's the thing",
  "Elevate your", "Unleash", "Experience the".
- Adverbs and intensifiers: really, truly, simply, effortlessly, seamlessly, perfectly.
- Buzzwords: premium, curated, bespoke, innovative, cutting-edge, world-class, seamless,
  hassle-free, one-stop, ultimate, unlock, empower, elevate, journey, solutions.
- "Not just X, but Y" and "X. Y. Z." staccato fragments as emphasis.
- Em dashes. Use a comma, a colon or a full stop.
- Exclamation marks anywhere on the page.
- Triplets by reflex ("fast, easy and secure"). Two things, or four, with substance.
- Lorem ipsum, "Your text here", "Coming soon", "Lorem", TODO.

Prefer: verbs over nouns ("we stitch" not "our stitching process"), the present tense,
sentences of varied length, and the customer as the subject ("you get", "your order").

## Nigerian context, when the spec is Nigerian
Naira with the ₦ sign and thousands separators. WhatsApp and calls as contact channels.
Real place names (Yaba, Ikeja, Lekki, Surulere, Victoria Island). Phone numbers as
0803 123 4567. "Pay on delivery" and "transfer" are normal payment words. Do not write
in Pidgin unless the spec asks; standard Nigerian English.

## Before you finish a page
Read every visible string once as the customer. Cut anything on the banned lists. Check:
headline says an outcome; every button is verb plus object; prices formatted; no
placeholder text; no two sections say the same thing; nothing claims what the spec
does not support.
